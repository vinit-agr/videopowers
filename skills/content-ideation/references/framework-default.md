# Content framework (default)

Setup copies this file into the workspace as `framework.md`. The company
then edits it freely; the workspace version always wins. It answers three
questions: which ideas, in what balance, and which first.

It joins three methods, each with one job:

| Method | Its job here |
|---|---|
| Schwartz's awareness ladder | Balance: reach buyers at every stage, not only the ones already shopping |
| Sheridan, *They Ask, You Answer* | Answer the questions buyers actually ask, honestly, and use the videos in sales |
| Hormozi, *$100M Leads* | Where ideas come from, lead magnets, and hook → retain → reward inside each video |

## Belief

One sentence every video supports, so many videos add up to one thing the
company stands for. It's about the viewer's world, not the product. A good
belief is something a buyer could agree with before they've heard of you.

Belief: <one sentence, or "not yet">

## Every video has two jobs

1. **Be found by unknown prospects:** people at a buyer's organization who
   have never heard of you. On YouTube this depends on the title and
   thumbnail earning clicks.
2. **Move known prospects forward:** sales sends the video before or after
   a call.

Most videos lean one way. The score (below) makes the lean visible, so a
month's plan serves both.

## The ladder and the buckets

Schwartz's seven steps of awareness, grouped into four buckets.

| Step | Bucket | The viewer | What the video must do | Story shape (one breath) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Unaware | problem | Sees no problem | Open with a moment they recognise, then show what it hides | "You think A. Actually B is happening, and it costs you C. Here's what to do today." |
| 2. Problem aware | problem | Knows it happens; hasn't sized it | Name it and put a number on it | (as step 1) |
| 3. Exploring fixes | options | Comparing approaches | Compare the real options fairly | "There are N ways to fix this. Here's what each really costs, who it suits, and the bar any of them must clear." |
| 4. Setting requirements | options | Has picked an approach | Give them the checklist for "good" | (as step 3) |
| 5. Choosing and checking | us | Knows you, not convinced | The product doing the hard thing, the proof, who it isn't for | "Here's our product doing the hard thing, the proof, and who it isn't for." |
| 6. Getting agreement | buying | Must sell it inside | Something to forward to the person who signs | "Here's what to take to the person who signs." |
| 7. Most aware | buying | Wants it, hasn't bought | State the offer plainly | "Here's the offer, plainly." |

Never invent a contrarian "actually". If the evidence has no real surprise,
use a different shape.

| Bucket | Steps | Target share |
|---|---|---|
| problem | 1–2 | about ⅓ |
| options | 3–4 | about ¼ |
| us | 5 | about ¼ |
| buying | 6–7 | about ⅙ |

These shares are a starting guess. Change them as you learn what works.

**Sharing out N slots.** M = videos already made or planned in the plan's
period; existing_b = those in bucket b.
1. need_b = max(0, share_b × (M + N) − existing_b).
2. Scale the need_b so they add up to N.
3. Give each bucket the whole part of its scaled need. Hand out the slots
   left over by largest remainder. Ties go to the bucket with fewer videos
   in the last 90 days, then to the buying end of the ladder.

Example: N = 4, M = 1 (one problem video already planned). Needs: problem
5/3 − 1 = 0.67, options 1.25, us 1.25, buying 0.83 (already adding up to 4).
Whole parts: 0, 1, 1, 0 → 2 slots left; the largest remainders go to buying
(0.83) and problem (0.67). Result: 1, 1, 1, 1.

Also check **product variety**: no product left out two periods running,
unless the profile says to focus on one.

## Where ideas come from

- **The topic list** (below): topics that exist before any brainstorm.
- **The idea log:** new questions first.
- **Hormozi's five sources:**
  - far past: the company's own history and experience;
  - recent past: documenting real work as it happens;
  - present: what buyers are asking now (the idea log);
  - trending: what the niche is talking about;
  - manufactured: your own studies and data.
- **Sheridan's Big 5** buyer questions, for any bucket: cost, problems,
  versus, reviews, best.

## Shaping an idea: five questions

1. **Which bucket, and which step?**
2. **Where does it come from?** The topic list, the idea log, or one of the
   five sources.
3. **Who is it for, in what moment?** One role, and the situation that
   makes them look (a new hire leaves, a budget review, a website redesign).
4. **Who will we send it to, and why would they reply?**
5. **What should they do next?** One step up the ladder, often a lead magnet
   from the profile. Clear, not clever.

## The gate

Does the idea serve the viewer and the belief? If it's mainly about the
company itself ("why we started", "our new office"), it becomes a short
post, not a pillar video. Score it only if it passes.

## The score

Write the title, first line and thumbnail concept **before** scoring, because
Click is scored on them. Five measures, each 1–3, each with one line of
reason.

| | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pain:** do buyers feel it? | Nice to know | A known problem | Costs them people or money this month |
| **Only us:** could a competitor say exactly this? | Anyone could | Our angle on a common topic | Our own data, research or experience |
| **Click:** would a stranger in the niche, scrolling, click this title and thumbnail? | Unlikely; vague or insider | Maybe; clear but not surprising | Yes: a number, a surprise or a question they need answered |
| **Sales use:** would sales send it to a prospect? | Rarely | Sometimes | Before most calls, or as a reason to reach out |
| **Cost:** how much work? | Days of research | About a day | Hours; we already know it |

Three numbers for each idea:
- **Reach** = Pain + Only us + Click (3–9): how well it finds unknown prospects.
- **Sales** = Pain + Only us + Sales use (3–9): how well it moves known prospects.
- **Total** = all five (5–15).

**Lean:** *leans reach* if Reach ≥ Sales + 2; *leans sales* if Sales ≥
Reach + 2; otherwise *both*.

| Total | Verdict |
|---|---|
| 12–15 | Make it |
| 10–11 | Make it if it fills an empty bucket or the mix needs its lean |
| 9 or less | Fix the angle, or drop it |

The score ranks ideas **within** a bucket, never across buckets; otherwise
problem ideas (low on sales use) would always lose to buying ideas. A low
Click with high Sales is often fixed by a better title, not by dropping the
idea. These thresholds are a starting guess; adjust after the first month.

## Lead magnets

A lead magnet is "a complete solution to a narrow problem" that reveals
another problem your product solves (Hormozi). Three kinds:
- **reveal a problem:** a self-check, a calculator, an audit;
- **a sample:** a small free piece of the product;
- **one step:** one step of a bigger process, done for them.

The video is never gated. List your magnets in the profile under *Next
steps and lead magnets*.

## Length types

| Length type | Used for | Words | At 170 wpm |
|---|---|---|---|
| `study` | Your own study or a data-heavy video | 1,800–2,200 | 11–13 min |
| `standard` | Most videos: problem, buyer question, versus, "us" | 1,500–1,800 | 9–11 min |
| `short` | Buying-stage videos (a business case, the offer) | 1,000–1,400 | 6–8 min |

The whole video counts: hook, chapters and close. Ideation sets the length
type; the outline may change it and says why.
