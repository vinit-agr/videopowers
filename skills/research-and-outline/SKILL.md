# research-and-outline

Turns one planned video into an **approved story outline**, with the
research that supports it. The research exists only to serve the story: if
there's no compelling story, there's no video.

```
content-ideation ─► research-and-outline ─► script-writing ─► record ─► …
                    (gate 1: the human writes "Approved")
```

A good story here is **simple** (one problem, one fix), **clear**,
**surprising** (one fact that makes the viewer stop), and **usable**: the
viewer can do something with it the same day. Think like a master
storyteller who refuses to make anything complicated.

## Before anything

1. Find the workspace: `../content-ideation/references/workspace.md`,
   *Finding the workspace*. None → offer its *Setup*.
2. Status sync (same file, *Approval words and status sync*).
3. Find the video's registry row and check its status:

   | Status | Do |
   |---|---|
   | `planned` | Start |
   | `researching`, `outlined` | Resume or revise (below) |
   | `approved` | Say it's approved. Revising sends it back to `outlined`: ask first |
   | `idea`, `proposed` | Stop: it isn't planned yet. Point to content-ideation (plan or test, then review) |
   | later than `approved` | Stop: the outline stage is done |

4. Read the video's `packaging.md`, the profile (especially *Research
   rules*, *Approved proof*, *Never claim*), the framework and
   `niche-words.md`.

## Resuming

Research and studies can take days. Read the registry note and the files in
the folder, and continue from the first missing piece. For a video built on
your own study: no `study/` files → start the gate; criteria not approved → stop again and ask; no
`results.md` → collect; no `outline.md` → write it; an outline exists →
revise it per the human's comments.

## Steps

1. **A first guess at the story, in one breath,** in the shape for its
   awareness step (framework.md, *The ladder and the buckets*). Write it at
   the top of `research.md` (or of `study/method.md` for a study). With no
   research at all, it goes straight into the outline's *The story in one
   breath*. No invented
   contrarian take: if there's no real "actually", don't fake one.
2. **Choose the kind of research:** desk, your own study, conversation prep,
   or none (`references/research-kinds.md`). "Research needed" in
   `packaging.md` is ideation's estimate: light desk research to find the
   surprise is always allowed; say so in the registry note. The research rules always
   apply. Your own study goes through the **study gate**: nothing is
   collected until the human approves `study/criteria.md`.
3. **Research to prove or kill the guess.** Look hardest for the surprise.
   If the evidence says the guess is wrong, change the story.
4. **Simplicity check.** One problem and one fix. More than about three big
   ideas, or more words than the length range → propose a part 1 and
   part 2 (`references/outline-format.md`).
5. **Write the outline** in `outline.md`
   (`references/outline-format.md`). Check it against the hook, retain and
   reward checklists in `references/hook-retain-reward.md`, then, at lower
   priority, the ideas in `references/structure-ideas.md`. A `conversation` video gets the
   conversation outline instead. The length type comes from the registry;
   change it only with a reason in the outline. Registry → `outlined`, with
   the version in the note.
6. **Refresh packaging.** Check the title, first line and thumbnail in
   `packaging.md` against the story. If the story moved, update them (and
   `thumbnail.html`; rebuild it from the concept if it's missing), add a `## Changes` line, and say what changed under
   the outline's title. If nothing changed, say "Packaging unchanged."
7. **Story review (gate 1).** Hand the outline to the human. Revise draft
   by draft (v0.1, v0.2…) until they write **Approved** as its first line.
   You never write it yourself. Status sync then sets `approved`.

When done, tell the human: the story in one breath, the surprise, the
length, and the open ⚠ items they need to answer.

## Later (not in v1)

- **Atomization:** cutting the chapters marked "stands alone: yes" into
  clips, posts and carousels.
- **A firmer hook framework**, grown from what works on published videos.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Instead |
|---|---|
| A contrarian "actually" the evidence doesn't support | Use the plain shape; say there's no surprise |
| A hook that lists the sections | Promise what they'll get; one open loop |
| A number with no source | Source it, mark it ⚠, or cut it |
| Guessing the speaker's story, result or opinion | Put it under *Needs from the speaker (⚠)* |
| Collecting study data before "Approved" on the criteria | Stop at the gate, every time |
| A mini hook forced onto "who is speaking" | "—"; stands alone: no |
| Squeezing an over-long story into the range | Propose part 1 and part 2 |
| Writing "Approved" yourself | Only the human writes it |
| Contacting anyone, or sending the guest invitation | Draft it for a person to send |
